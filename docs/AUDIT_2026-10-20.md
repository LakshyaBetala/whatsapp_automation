# ASVA full audit, 20 October 2026

Evidence comes from the live database (Supabase, last 120 days), the live
server (Google Cloud, `asva-gcp-1`), the code at commit `8eb76d5`, and the live
website. Severity: **P0** loses money or trust today, **P1** breaks a promised
feature, **P2** quality or cost.

## The five things that matter

1. **Fewer than half of real reminders reach customers (about 46%).** Jul-Oct:
   659 delivered, 482 expired, 244 failed. The shop's WhatsApp runs on the shop
   laptop; when the laptop is off, sends wait 72 hours and expire.
2. **ASVA thinks reminders went out when they did not.** 894 cadence markers say
   "sent" for 380 customers whose real message expired or failed, so their
   schedule moved on as if they had been reminded.
3. **"Bill sent as soon as it is made" effectively does not work.** 1,299 new
   bills Jul-Oct, 17 bill messages attempted, 4 sent. A bill only goes out if
   the owner manually exports each invoice as a PDF into a folder.
4. **Renewals are manual, so shops silently lapse.** 1 of 13 shops is paying and
   active. Renewal = owner pays a UPI link, then the founder marks it by hand.
   Two trials ended 19 and 20 Oct.
5. **The owner never heard from ASVA for three weeks.** The bot WhatsApp was
   disconnected from about 1 Oct to 20 Oct (fixed 20 Oct), so customer replies
   like "not our pls check" never reached the owner.

## A. WhatsApp delivery and transport (P0)

| Evidence | Value |
|---|---|
| Real reminders Jul-Oct (excluding markers) | 659 sent, 482 expired, 244 failed, 49 queued |
| August alone | 181 sent, 332 expired |
| Rishab Oct 12-17 | agent offline and WhatsApp down every day |

**Root cause.** Each shop's WhatsApp (Baileys, port 3001) lives inside the
desktop app on the shop laptop. Laptops sleep, shut down at night, and stay off
on Sundays and holidays. The server queues the message; nothing can send it.

**Fix.** Run each shop's WhatsApp session on the server (section Q has the ban
analysis, section H the RAM). The laptop keeps only Tally sync.

Pros of moving to the server:
- Reminders go out at the planned hour whether the laptop is on or off.
- Customer replies are read 24/7, which the AI replies need.
- One place to monitor, reconnect and upgrade Baileys; no desktop release
  needed to fix WhatsApp.
- The desktop app gets lighter (no Node WhatsApp process on old shop PCs).

Cons and mitigations:
- The server holds every shop's WhatsApp login. Encrypt session files at rest,
  keep the server closed (it already allows only SSH key + Tailscale), keep
  encrypted bundles off-box.
- One server outage stops all shops. Today it already does (the scheduler lives
  there); add an external uptime monitor and keep the 20-minute bundle restore.
- Many accounts behind one cloud IP. Use the Mumbai region (same country as the
  phones), stable device identity, no frequent relinks. Risk not measurable in
  advance; watch for 403/forbidden and stop that shop's sends at once.
- Each shop scans a QR once more (inside the desktop app, served by the server).
- Owners may fear "my WhatsApp is on your server". WhatsApp's Linked Devices
  list shows ASVA and the owner can unlink it any time; say that on screen.

## B. Reminder engine (P0)

- **False "sent" markers.** `app/jobs/reminder_sweep.py:562` treats `queued` as
  done and writes `cadence_marker` rows with `delivery_status="sent"` at queue
  time. When the outbox row later expires or fails, the markers stay. 894 such
  markers, 380 customers. Fix: markers carry the outbox row id and stay
  `queued`; delivery flips them to `sent`; expiry or failure deletes them so the
  cadence point fires again (respecting the daily cap).
- **Inflated statistics.** Markers are `type='reminder'`, so every count of
  "reminders sent" (Health, traffic, analytics, Command Center) includes them.
  The real reminder volume is about a quarter of what is shown. Fix: exclude
  `template_name='cadence_marker'` everywhere, or move markers to their own table.
- **Consolidation works.** One combined message per party per run
  (`manual_remind_hi`); the "11 a day" pattern was markers, not messages.

## C. Bill on creation (P1)

- `tally_agent/agent.py:20-54` attaches a PDF only if `Sales_<no>.pdf` exists in
  a folder the owner exports to by hand. `app/routers/tally.py:719-749` sends
  only when that PDF is present. Result: 4 bills sent out of 1,299.
- Fix: on every new sales bill for a customer with a number, send a text bill
  notice (invoice number, date, amount, due date, pay link) with no PDF needed;
  attach the PDF when one exists. To limit volume and ban risk, combine a
  customer's bills of the day into one message sent at the end of the day (or
  within the next send window).

## D. Customer replies and AI accuracy (P1)

Live test on the server, 20 Oct (synthetic messages):

| Message | Current AI | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| "5 tareek ko payment kar dunga" | promise, 2026-11-05 | correct |
| "Nill balance" | paid_claim (pauses reminders) | dispute: tell the owner |
| "kitna baki hai bhai" | unclear | balance question: answer it |
| "10 box wire bhejo kal" | chatter | order: ignore for payments |

- Stored intents are lost: `conversations.record` writes the row before
  classification and never updates it (all text rows have `intent = NULL`).
- Customers' own WhatsApp Business auto-greetings ("Welcome to Shanti Group")
  are stored as replies.
- No Tamil in the prompt although the shops and customers are in Tamil Nadu.
- `gemini-2.5-flash` runs with default thinking: 2.4-4 s per message and extra
  output tokens. Classification needs no thinking.
- Paid claims: 5 of 7 ended "broken" (customer said paid, money never matched).

## E. Owner bot (P2)

- `app/services/bot.py` is 2,313 lines with about 30 commands (LIST, BILL, PAID,
  STOP, START, CHASE, LETTER, REMIND, DIGEST, SENT, TERMS, SCORE, FORECAST,
  CARD, CHECK, RECOVERED, PROMISES, ...). Owners do not remember commands.
- Fix: understand plain language and voice notes (Gemini reads audio), answer
  with numbered choices, keep the old commands working.

## F. Pay link insights (P2)

- 28 opens, 7 "Pay" taps, 0 confirmed. Taps expire (2), get dismissed (2) or
  superseded (3). Nothing links a tap to the Tally receipt that follows days
  later outside the 3-day window, so the shop never sees "the link worked".
- Fix: match taps to receipts for 14 days, show "paid via your link" on Today,
  and count link-driven recovery in the monthly report.

## H. Server and infrastructure (P1)

- Real memory use is about 700 MB of 3.9 GB; the "3.2 GB" figure is *available*
  memory. The rest is Linux file cache, freed on demand.
- Bot WhatsApp process 90 MB, plus a 53 MB `npm start` wrapper that does nothing
  (run `node index.js` directly).
- Estimated RAM per extra shop session in one shared Node process: 20-40 MB.
  13 shops: about 1.2 GB total. 50 shops: about 2-2.5 GB. **6 GB is not needed.**
- No external uptime monitor: the old box died on about 18 Oct and nobody was
  told (its own alert emails died with it). Add a free external monitor on
  `/health` that emails and WhatsApps the founder.
- Backups (`pg_dump` nightly) are on the same box. Copy them off-box daily.
- GCP live prices (INR, Mumbai): E2 core Rs 2.51/h, RAM Rs 0.337/GB-h, balanced
  disk Rs 11.52/GB-month: about Rs 3,300-3,800/month, covered by the trial.
- After the trial: Kamatera lists about $19/month for 2 vCPU / 4 GB burstable
  (CPU above 10% average billed extra) and less for 2 GB. Measured use fits
  2-4 GB. Confirm on Kamatera's calculator before moving (target around day 80,
  about 8 Jan 2027).

## I. Security (P1)

Good: ufw default-deny (only SSH key and Tailscale), backend 8000 not exposed,
bot 3002 bound to localhost, RLS on every public table, no secrets in git.

- **Public `invoices` storage bucket**: invoice PDFs readable by anyone with the
  link. Make it private and serve signed URLs.
- **No rate limiting** on public endpoints (`/pay`, pairing codes, `/tally/*`).
- **Dashboard auth is the agent token in the URL** (17 admin routes, 4 mobile
  routes); Command Center uses `?key=`. URLs leak through history, screenshots
  and logs. Move to a session cookie after one exchange.

## J. Subscriptions, renewal and payment method (P0 business)

- Status today: Rishab active (annual, to 2027-10-12); 10 suspended; 2 trials
  ended 19-20 Oct; every shop is on the legacy `pro` plan.
- Renewal is manual. Fix: Razorpay Subscriptions with UPI AutoPay (about 2% +
  GST; under Rs 15,000 the customer approves once, then it debits monthly) with
  a webhook that renews automatically. Needs a Razorpay account with your KYC.
  Interim without KYC: annual plans via a payment link, renewed by webhook.

## O. Supabase strategy

- 25 MB database of 500 MB free; 0.8 MB of storage. Not a cost or capacity
  issue for years at this size.
- Risks: free projects pause after 7 days idle (the app's own traffic and the
  6-hourly keep-alive prevent it); no automatic backups on free (fix with the
  off-box `pg_dump`).
- Plan: stay on free; move to Pro ($25/month) only past about 300 MB, or when
  daily managed backups are worth paying for. Do not self-host Postgres now.

## P. AI provider strategy

- Volume is tiny: 17 customer replies in 60 days. Even 50 shops x 200 replies a
  month is about 10 million tokens: a few dollars a month on Flash-Lite.
- The Google Cloud trial credit **cannot** pay for the Gemini Developer API (AI
  Studio key) but **can** pay for Gemini on Vertex AI.
- Plan: rules first (PAID, numbers, "kitna", "bill bhejo", STOP) handle most
  replies at zero cost; Gemini Flash-Lite on Vertex AI (paid by the trial
  credit, a server service account, no key needed) for the rest; the existing
  AI Studio free key as fallback; if both fail, stay silent and tell the owner.
- Note: on the AI Studio free tier Google may use prompts to improve its
  products; Vertex AI does not. Customer payment messages belong on Vertex.

## Q. WhatsApp ban risk

- Baileys is an unofficial client; WhatsApp can ban a number permanently with
  no appeal. Vendor blogs claim high ban rates for bulk senders; ASVA's profile
  is lower risk (existing customers, transactional, low volume, replies happen)
  but not zero.
- Main triggers: customers blocking or reporting, bursts, identical text, cold
  numbers, low reply rate, frequent relinking.
- Guardrails: random 20-60 s gaps (exists), daily caps (exists), warm-up for a
  newly linked number, only saved customers with outstanding, phrasing variation,
  slow down a party after 3 unanswered reminders, instant STOP (exists), stop a
  shop's sends on 403/forbidden and alert the owner.
- The ban-proof path: the official WhatsApp Cloud API with Coexistence (same
  number, the owner keeps using the WhatsApp Business app) at about Rs 0.115 per
  utility message in India. Needs Meta business verification and approved
  templates per shop. Keep the transport pluggable and pilot it with one shop.
